## Footnotes

`<Footnote>` sits inline at the point of the reference. Its content becomes the note at the bottom of the page. The renderer numbers notes 1, 2, 3 in document order.

```vue
<Paragraph>
  Revenue grew 12%<Footnote>Compared with the same quarter last year.</Footnote> across all regions.
</Paragraph>
```

A footnote is a block container. Loose text becomes a paragraph, and you can use `<Paragraph>`s inside for several paragraphs or formatting:

```vue
<Footnote>
  <Paragraph>Source: internal ledger.</Paragraph>
  <Paragraph italic>Figures are rounded.</Paragraph>
</Footnote>
```

A footnote can't sit inside a header, a footer or another footnote.

## Table of contents

```vue
<TableOfContents title="Contents" :levels="[1, 3]" hyperlinks />
```

It lists paragraphs that use [headings](/guides/text-and-paragraphs#headings), from level 1 to level 3 by default. `levels` is `[first, last]`. Entries are links unless you pass `:hyperlinks="false"`.

Word does not know the page numbers until it lays the document out. The file asks Word to update its fields when it opens, so Word shows a prompt and fills the table in after you accept. Other apps may show the table empty until you update fields by hand.

The [preview](/guides/preview) shows the headings the table would list, without page numbers.
